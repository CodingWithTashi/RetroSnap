# Camera lineup

The plan for growing RetroSnap from 5 cameras to the 38 in Dazz Cam's lineup, taken from the six screenshots in `docs/app-camera-video-list/` (the reference app's photo row and video row, captured 12 October 2026; app version not recorded). Read this before adding or changing a camera. The brief behind it is `docs/research-brief.md`.

## Goal

**Every camera's output matches the reference app's by measurement, and beats it in quality.** "Match" is the brief's definition: measured perceptual parity on tone curve, colour rendering, grain, halation, vignette, aberration, softness, light leaks, frames, aspect ratio and date stamp. "Beat" is its "Exceed" list: full-resolution output, viewfinder equals export, banding-free gradients, highlight roll-off, resolution-independent grain, metadata kept, battery and thermal behaviour.

Everything is ours: recipes, shaders, icons, frames, grain and leak textures. The names are the one exception: at the owner's direction (12 October 2026, D6) a camera is named in the app exactly as the reference app names it.

**The UI and UX do not change in functionality.** A new camera is an entry in the existing carousel, a `CameraStyle` case and a `CameraLook` recipe. Cameras that would need more than that are flagged under "Decisions".

## How to use this file

- Work goes in batches of five. Batch 1 is the five cameras with reference material in hand; after that, the five whose references arrive next, in the suggested order under "Batches".
- One batch at a time: add the five, build, run the tests, check each camera with `-rsScreen look -rsCamera <id>`, compare with the references, tick them off here.
- `id` is the `CameraStyle` raw value. It is written to disk (`library.json` and the user defaults), so once a camera has shipped its id never changes. Names, badges and summaries can change freely.
- A camera's name in the app is its Reference name, spelled exactly as in the tables (D6, decided by the owner on 12 October 2026). The "Name (proposal)" column is kept only as the fallback should the owner decide otherwise for a batch. The unit tests and UI tests assert on camera names, so a rename touches them.

## Ground rules

1. **Reference photos, not reference files.** A look is matched by comparing RetroSnap's output with the reference app's output for the same input. We measure and imitate the result; we never open the other app's binary or assets, and we never ship a LUT or texture lifted from its files. A camera's colour is a 3D lookup table *measured from the photos* (D7): estimated by least squares from the pairs, smoothed, and held to our parametric fit wherever the pictures showed no colour, which is our own model of the measurements, not the reference app's table.
2. **Colour and tone are matched by measurement; textures are ours.** Grain, leaks, dust, frames and stamps are generated or drawn by us and are allowed to be better than the reference.
3. **A camera is a `CameraStyle` case plus a `CameraLook` recipe.** The pipeline grows new layers when a batch needs them ("Pipeline work"), but a camera never gets a view of its own.
4. **Capture modes are not looks.** D Half, D3D, Golf and Slide P need more than a recipe (two shots in one frame, a three-frame wiggle, a grid of frames, a projector playback). They are listed, but each needs a decision before its batch, because it changes what the shutter does.

## Reference material (`ref/`, gitignored except its README)

How the references are made: a stock iPhone Camera photo is **imported into the reference app**, each camera is applied to it, and the result is either **screenshotted** (batch 1: the reference app did not save without its paid tier) or, since batch 2, **saved as the reference app's own export** (full resolution, with the input's EXIF). Colour spaces: an iPhone's photos and screenshots are tagged Display P3, and so are some of the reference app's exports (Inst C, Classic U, Inst SQC); others come untagged (D Classic, CT2F, and CPM35's `output_1.jpg`) and are taken as sRGB, which is how the phone shows an untagged JPEG and so what the owner holds RetroSnap against (the untagged and tagged exports share every JPEG parameter but the profile segment, so they are the same app's output through two paths; the EXIF colour-space tag is "uncalibrated" on both). The app's shaders work on sRGB values (SwiftUI's colour effects and the Core Image context both run in sRGB), so every picture is converted to sRGB before it is measured or fitted (`tools/fit.py` does it, and `tools/refcrop.py` keeps the profile on a crop so that it can; the pairs under `ref/pairs` are sRGB). The first fits read the P3 values as sRGB, which made every saturated colour come out duller on a phone than in the measurements; that is fixed, and the P3-valued pairs are kept under `ref/pairs/p3/`.

What a screenshot is good for: tone, colour, frames and layout, at screen resolution (the picture is about 900 px wide). What it is not good for: grain structure and fine texture, which need a saved export. An export (batch 2's references, and CPM35's second picture) is good for everything: the canvas is the whole file, grain and softness are measured at full resolution (`tools/grain.py`, `tools/softness.py`), and the stamp at its true size (`tools/stamp.py`). The result screenshots carry the reference app's banner and close button: crop to the picture before measuring. Whether the reference app's preview equals its export is still unknown for batch 1's cameras (no export of them); batch 2 is matched to the exports.

The first input is landscape and the other two portrait, so every canvas has been seen both ways: the reference app lays the strip, the 6×7 rebate, the compact's 3:2 crop and the mini print's sheet down for a landscape picture and stands them up for a portrait one, turns the slide mount's window but not the mount, and keeps the square frames and the square print the same way up whatever the picture. A film camera's date back turns with the film: it runs down the left edge of a portrait picture, as a real one would. "Frames" and "Stamps" below have the measurements.

## Pipeline work

New layers, and the batch that introduces each. A new layer is added in both renderers (`PhotoLookView` and `LookRenderer.render`, same order) and, if it needs a shader uniform, in all four of `RetroLook.metal`, `LookShaders`, `LookRenderer`, `LookUniforms`.

`tools/stamp.py` finds the ink under its screen blend wherever it lands: over a dark background as red, over a bright one as the pink lift a screened red leaves (red saturates, green and blue rise by 0.72 of their distance to white), keeping only glyph-sized pieces that stand in one row (a warm lamp or a red pepper in the corner fooled the first version), and reads RetroSnap's 1200-px exports without the opening that erased their strokes.

## Decisions

Each is needed before the batch named. Recommendations are defaults: a session may take them without asking unless the owner has said otherwise.

- **D1 (batch 1) The design HTML.** `CLAUDE.md` says looks are transcribed from the `LOOKS` table in `docs/retrosnap-mobile-app-design/project/Photo.dc.html`. Options: extend that table for every camera, or let the app lead from here and keep the HTML as the record of the first five. Recommendation: the app leads. **Taken in batch 1:** the app leads; the HTML is the record of the first five and is not extended.
- **D2 (batch 1) Picture ratio and canvas.** Cameras whose output is not the plain 3:4 photo (3:2 in a strip, 3:2 in a square mount, square, 4:5): draw the picture at its ratio inside RetroSnap's photo with the canvas around it, no viewfinder change. Recommendation: that. **Taken in batch 1, amended 13 October 2026:** the canvas follows the `framed` flag exactly like the Instant print (the gallery tile and the collage show the whole photo; the Result, Viewer and Edit screens and the saved file show the canvas with the picture in its window; the viewfinder dims a band to hint at the ratio), but the canvas has the camera's own shape as the reference app lays it out, not the photo's box: the owner compared the first version with the reference app side by side and the frames did not match, because a strip drawn into a 3:4 box is not the strip the reference app draws around a 3:2 picture. A camera with no canvas in the reference (CPM35) shows the 3:2 crop with nothing around it, as the reference does.
- **D3 (batches 4, 5, 7) Capture modes.** D Half (two shots into one frame), D3D (three-frame wiggle played as an animation), Golf (a grid of sequential frames), Slide P (projector playback of photos). Each changes the shutter or the result screen, which is a functionality change. Options per camera: skip, ship as a look only (D Half with a half-frame canvas and one shot), or build the mode. Recommendation: look only, and revisit.
- **D4 (batch 6) Video cameras.** Give `CameraStyle` a family (photo or video) so the carousel shows the right ones in each `CaptureMode`, or let every camera do both. Recommendation: a family, matching the reference app's two rows, no new UI.
- **D5 Original / Original V.** A no-look camera is cheap (an empty recipe). Recommendation: add `plain`; decide `plainVideo` with D4.
- **D6 Names.** Confirm the names before each batch. **Taken in batch 1:** the owner wants the app to use the reference app's names, exactly: 135 NE, CPM35, D Slide, S 67, S Classic. From here the Reference column is the name and the proposal column is the fallback. **Taken in batch 2:** a re-tuned camera takes the reference name with its look (35mm Film → D Classic, Instant → Inst C); Disposable and Digicam keep the design's names and looks until their references arrive, because a camera named after the reference app's should look like it.

- **D7 (13 October 2026, the owner) The colour model.** The owner asked for the closest match possible after the face still rendered 2 to 4 L darker than the reference's on every camera: that lift is a property of the face's colours (the same input colours elsewhere in the picture get it too), so a per-colour model can give it, and the parametric one could not however it was weighted. **Taken:** a camera's colour is a 3D lookup table measured from its pairs (`tools/lut.py`), with the parametric fit as its prior; the table is ours, estimated from photographs, never the reference app's.
- **D8 (batch 2) Untagged exports.** Some of the reference app's exports carry no colour profile. **Taken:** they are sRGB (see "Reference material": same encoder, and the phone shows them as sRGB, which is what the owner compares with).
- **D10 (refinement round, 13 October 2026) The slide mount's card.** The four new pictures showed the grey card on a portrait picture and two cards batch 1 had not seen (a printed cream card and a white plastic mount), so the card does not follow the picture's orientation as batch 1 had it. **Taken:** as D9: every design seen is drawn and one is picked per photo by a stable hash of its id (`SlideMountDesign.forPhoto`, salted so it varies independently of the square print's card: `FrameDesign`), and the reference exports in `ReferenceExportTests` get the card the reference app gave that picture.
- **D9 (batch 2) A canvas the reference app varies.** The square print's card changes from print to print (three designs seen on three pictures), as the slide mount's card did in batch 1. **Taken:** draw every design seen and pick one per photo by a stable hash of its id (`SquarePrintDesign.forPhoto`), so the gallery varies as the reference app's does and a photo always comes out the same; the reference exports in `ReferenceExportTests` are given the card the reference app gave that picture, so the sheet compares like with like.

#### What the refinement round taught (13–14 October 2026)

- **Cropping the reference's screens.** `refcrop.py`'s edge snap fails on a dark picture against the black film strip (the tungsten interior, the backlit window), and matching the input's content fails because the reference app zooms its picture in by about 12% inside the frame; so one canvas rectangle per camera and orientation is measured on the bright picture (the wall) and cut `--exact` on the others (`tools/pairs.py` holds them). A slide mount photographed at an angle (the market, −2.0°) is cut at its measured angle; its colour median did not move, so the market's residual is colour, not geometry. The date backs' screened ink over a bright background is a pink lift (red ≥ 238 and red − green > half of 255 − green), and only glyph-sized pieces in one row are ink (warm lamps and red peppers fooled the first mask).
- **Compute.** A fit subprocess spawns eight BLAS threads; several at once thrashed the machine ten times slower (a wall pair took 25 CPU-minutes, alone and single-threaded two). Every fit now runs with `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1` (`refpairs.py`, `refit.py`, `loo.py`). `fit.py` keeps only tile-sized pairs for its report and `lut.py` measures on its box samples, so seven 10 MP pairs fit in memory. The loops run niced, so an `xcodebuild test` on the same machine starves them (half a CPU-minute in a quarter of an hour); three `halation.py --fine --wide` grids (224 settings by seven pairs) sharing 16 GB with the swap full took two hours of wall time each; the grid now caches the glow's blur per threshold and radius (`spread_of`: amount and tint only scale it, identical to 3e-16), which cuts a grid by more than half. The venv's Python is Xcode's 3.9: nested double quotes inside an f-string are a syntax error there, and one such edit to `recipe.py` at midnight crashed six loops at their last step after their fits had finished (`refine.py --resume` rebuilds a loop's summary from its files).
- **The grain's weight by luminance.** Scaled by its peak, the reference's grain runs 0.53, 0.85, 0.99, 0.97, 0.87, 0.63, 0.38, 0.20 over eight luminance bands (`tools/grain_profile.py`); the shader's `4 l (1 − l)` peaked at mid grey and was replaced by `0.2 + 0.8 l^0.7 (1 − l)^1.5 / 0.252`, which matches within 0.05 per band. `FilmGrain.amount` is now the amplitude at the peak (luminance about 0.32), and five cameras' amounts were re-measured under the new weight.
- **The floor.** `tools/floor.py` gives each pair the median scatter of the reference around the mean colour it gives each input colour: what no colour-to-colour model can beat. On the fitted pictures the batch recipes already sat on it; on the market they sat 0.7 to 4.8 above it. The gate for a camera on a picture is now its median at most 0.5 over that floor.
- **The market is unseen colour, not a layer.** `tools/residual.py` maps what the table leaves over position, luminance and hue; on no camera and no picture is the radial or tilt residual above the reference's own local variation, so there is no vignette shape, leak, fall-off or edge softness left to model. What remains is a hue sector on the market (its turquoise crates, purple cabbage and lemons are under 0.5% of the fitted pictures' pixels) and, on the slide-like cameras, a tone gap in the deepest shadows. The reference does not adapt exposure to the picture: the per-picture mean tone shifts after the fit are within ±1.3 L and have no common direction across cameras.
- **The leave-one-out rule.** "Raise the prior until the held-out median is within 0.5 of the fitted one" shrinks the gap mostly by fitting the seen pictures worse: for 135 NE each doubling of the prior cost 0.1 to 0.3 on every fitted picture and changed the held-out medians by 0.05, and for Inst C and Inst SQC the raise made both worse. The measure of generalisation is the held-out median itself (what a new picture will get), so `tools/pickprior.py` chooses each camera's prior from the leave-one-out runs by the smallest mean held-out median, the lower prior on a tie.
- **The devices.** The simulator `Retrosnap 16e` is the design's 390×844 points exactly; an iPhone 15 is 393×852 with a 59 pt safe top, and `DesignLayout` keeps the viewer's 104 pt from the safe area, so `screen_vs_bake.py` takes `--points 393 852 --safe-top 59` for a phone's screenshot.

## Measuring a pair

The scripts live in `tools/` (Python; make a venv with `python3 -m venv venv && venv/bin/pip install numpy pillow scipy`, and run them with `venv/bin/python -I`). The loop for one camera:

1. `tools/refscan.py` and `tools/refcrop.py` find the picture inside the reference screenshot and save the crop, with its colour profile (the result screen's banner, close button and the camera's own canvas are outside it). For an export of a print, `tools/printscan.py` measures the sheet, the print and the window first (and the paper's colours, corners and shadow for the artwork), and the crop is the window; a bare 3:2 export is its own crop. `tools/stamp.py` measures a date back and prints the `--mask` that keeps it out of the fit. Since the refinement round the pairs are listed in `tools/pairs.py` (the input, the reference file, the crop rectangle or window, the mask) and `tools/refpairs.py crop` cuts them all; a screenshot's rectangle is snapped on a bright picture and cut as given (`--exact`) on a dark one, because a dark picture against black film has no edge to snap to and the reference app lays a given input shape out the same way every time.
2. `tools/fit.py <id> ref/photo-input/input.JPG <crop> ref/pairs` (`tools/refpairs.py align` runs it for every pair at once, with a small sample count because a single-picture fit is only a bound) converts both pictures to sRGB, aligns the crop with the input (scale and offset, by the correlation of luminance gradients), writes the pair at the reference's resolution (`ref/pairs/<id>-input.png`, `<id>-reference.png`), fits the colour stage (matrix, curves, hue terms) plus the look's vignette by least squares on CIELAB differences, and writes `<id>.json` (the numbers for `CameraLook`) and `<id>-report.png` (input | fit | reference, with the curves). `--mask` keeps a date stamp out of the fit, `--inset` the mount's shadow, `--reg` pulls the matrix toward identity, `--hue-reg` the hue terms toward doing nothing.
3. `tools/sanity.py ref/pairs/<id>.json` runs the fit over a hue sweep and a grey ramp: a fit that swings a hue it never saw by tens of degrees wants more `--reg`.
4. Transcribe the JSON into the camera's `ColorStage` in `CameraLook.swift`. Then `tools/lut.py <id> ref/pairs --pair ref/pairs/<id> [--pair …] --halation <threshold,radius,amount,tint> [--weight …] --prior 0.3 --smooth 2 --skin 3` fits the table over the same pairs (after the camera's halation, since the table sits behind the glow in the pipeline), writes `ref/pairs/<id>.cube` and `<id>-lut.json`, and the `.cube` goes to `Retrosnap/Resources/Looks/<id>.cube` with `colorCube: "<id>"` on the recipe; `compare.py` then measures parity against the table.
5. `xcodebuild test … -only-testing:RetrosnapTests/ReferencePairTests` renders every pair through the real pipeline (`LookRenderer`, at the size the Viewer gives the picture) into `ref/pairs/<id>-retrosnap.png`. The test is skipped on a machine without `ref/`.
6. `tools/compare.py ref/pairs/<id>` prints the median and 95th percentile ΔE2000 of RetroSnap against the reference, of the fit against the reference, and of RetroSnap against the fit (pipeline parity). `tools/grain.py ref/pairs/<id>` measures the grain of the reference and of RetroSnap's render in the same flat blocks, and their ratio at mid grey: the reference's texture is what `FilmGrain` is tuned to. It also prints the detail each keeps where the input has some, in the middle and at the corners; `tools/softness.py <id> ref/pairs/<id> …` turns that into the camera's `blurRadius` (and says whether the corners want a `Softness` on top). `tools/halation.py ref/pairs/<id> ref/pairs/<id>-1 [--fine]` fits the camera's `Halation` from the same pairs: the glow the reference spreads around highlights, as the threshold, radius, amount and tint that best explain what the colour-only fit leaves next to bright pixels. `tools/stamp.py` run on RetroSnap's export checks a date back the same way it measured the reference's.

A camera with several pictures (`<id>`, `<id>-1`, `<id>-2`, … under "Re-verifying a shipped batch with a new picture"; since batch 2 every camera has three):

- Each new picture is cropped and aligned on its own with steps 1 and 2, under its pair name (`tools/fit.py <id>-<n> ref/photo-input/input_<n>.* <crop> ref/pairs …`). The single-picture fit this writes is only a bound: what the colour stage could reach on that picture alone. A canvas the reference app lays at an angle (the D Slide mount) is turned straight first with `tools/refcrop.py … --deskew`.
- The camera's fit is one colour stage over every pair at once: `tools/fit.py <id> <outdir> --pair ref/pairs/<id> --pair ref/pairs/<id>-1 --reg <r>`. Each pair counts the same whatever its size, so the recipe is as right on one picture as on the other; a pair's mask and inset come from its own JSON. When a camera cannot meet the tolerance on every picture, `--weight <pair>=w` leans the fit toward the picture that misses, and the fit kept is the one with the smallest worst median. The chosen fit's JSON and report are copied to `ref/pairs/<id>.json` and `<id>-report.png`, replacing the single-picture fit, so `tools/compare.py` measures parity against what is transcribed.
- `ReferencePairTests` renders every pair a camera has, and `tools/compare.py ref/pairs/<id> ref/pairs/<id>-1` and `tools/grain.py` measure each. Every pair must be within the tolerances.

Tolerances, set in batch 1 from what screenshots allow: **median ΔE2000 ≤ 3.0** for RetroSnap against the reference over the 4×4 box-filtered picture, **≤ 1.0** for RetroSnap against the Python fit (parity), and grain amplitude within a factor of 1.5 of the reference's high-pass standard deviation at mid grey. The grain left after the box filter is worth about 0.1 of the median on both sides of the comparison, so a camera at 3.0 ± 0.1 is at the line, not over it (batch 1 on its first picture: `negative` measured 3.02, the other four 1.8 to 2.7; after the second picture and the refit on both, four cameras measure 2.0 to 2.9 on both pictures and `negative` 3.5 and 3.6; after the move to sRGB and the hue terms, the fits are `plastic35` 2.4/2.3, `tlr` 1.9/2.6, `mediumFormat` 2.6/2.9, `slide` 3.5/2.5 and `negative` 3.4/3.6, each the fit with the smallest worse median). Half of what is left is texture, not colour: with both pictures blurred by 16 px the same fits measure 1.2 to 2.7, so the colour mapping is closer than the box-filtered median says. The floor for any per-pixel colour model on these screenshots, the scatter of the reference around the mean of each input colour, is 2.0 to 3.3; the measured tables (13 October) sit on it: `tlr` 1.7/2.3, `plastic35` 2.0/2.2, `mediumFormat` 2.2/2.6, `slide` 2.5/2.2, `negative` 2.6/3.2. A camera with several pictures must meet them on every picture. Batch 2, on three full-resolution exports each: `film` 1.6/1.5/1.8, `instSquare` 1.9/1.7/2.4, `compactPro` 2.4/2.6/3.0, `inst` 2.5/3.0/2.1, `classicSLR` 2.8/3.0/3.1 (the sign picture at the line, with grain about 0.1 of it); parity 0.5 to 0.7 for the film cameras and 0.9 to 1.15 for the two prints and Classic U, where the "fit" side of the comparison is the table on the sharp input while the render carries the camera's blur and grain, which is the caveat on that number for a blurred camera. Vignette fall-off is fitted as part of the colour stage (the look's radial gradient, with its amount as the free parameter). The screenshot is a preview at screen resolution; whether the reference app's export differs is unknown (D: no export available), so these numbers are against its preview.
