# Camera lineup

The plan for growing RetroSnap from 5 cameras to the 38 in Dazz Cam's lineup, taken from the six screenshots in `docs/app-camera-video-list/` (the reference app's photo row and video row, captured 12 October 2026; app version not recorded). Read this before adding or changing a camera. The brief behind it is `docs/research-brief.md`.

## Goal

**Every camera's output matches the reference app's by measurement, and beats it in quality.** "Match" is the brief's definition: measured perceptual parity on tone curve, colour rendering, grain, halation, vignette, aberration, softness, light leaks, frames, aspect ratio and date stamp. "Beat" is its "Exceed" list: full-resolution output, viewfinder equals export, banding-free gradients, highlight roll-off, resolution-independent grain, metadata kept, battery and thermal behaviour.

Everything is ours: recipes, shaders, icons, frames, grain and leak textures. The names are the one exception: at the owner's direction (12 October 2026, D6) a camera is named in the app exactly as the reference app names it.

**The UI and UX do not change in functionality.** A new camera is an entry in the existing carousel, a `CameraStyle` case and a `CameraLook` recipe. Cameras that would need more than that are flagged under "Decisions".

## How to use this file

- Work goes in batches of five. Batch 1 is the five cameras with reference material in hand; after that, the five whose references arrive next, in the suggested order under "Batches".
- One batch at a time: add the five, build, run the tests, check each camera with `-rsScreen look -rsCamera <id>`, compare with the references, tick them off here.
- `id` is the `CameraStyle` raw value. It is written to disk (`library.json` and the user defaults), so once a camera has shipped its id never changes. Names, badges and summaries can change freely.
- A camera's name in the app is its Reference name, spelled exactly as in the tables (D6, decided by the owner on 12 October 2026). The "Name (proposal)" column is kept only as the fallback should the owner decide otherwise for a batch. The unit tests and UI tests assert on camera names, so a rename touches them.

## Ground rules

1. **Reference photos, not reference files.** A look is matched by comparing RetroSnap's output with the reference app's output for the same input. We measure and imitate the result; we never open the other app's binary or assets, and we never ship a LUT or texture lifted from its files. A camera's colour is a 3D lookup table *measured from the photos* (D7): estimated by least squares from the pairs, smoothed, and held to our parametric fit wherever the pictures showed no colour, which is our own model of the measurements, not the reference app's table.
2. **Colour and tone are matched by measurement; textures are ours.** Grain, leaks, dust, frames and stamps are generated or drawn by us and are allowed to be better than the reference.
3. **A camera is a `CameraStyle` case plus a `CameraLook` recipe.** The pipeline grows new layers when a batch needs them ("Pipeline work"), but a camera never gets a view of its own.
4. **Capture modes are not looks.** D Half, D3D, Golf and Slide P need more than a recipe (two shots in one frame, a three-frame wiggle, a grid of frames, a projector playback). They are listed, but each needs a decision before its batch, because it changes what the shutter does.

## Reference material (`ref/`, gitignored except its README)

How the references are made: a stock iPhone Camera photo is **imported into the reference app**, each camera is applied to it, and the result is either **screenshotted** (batch 1: the reference app did not save without its paid tier) or, since batch 2, **saved as the reference app's own export** (full resolution, with the input's EXIF). So:

Colour spaces: an iPhone's photos and screenshots are tagged Display P3, and so are some of the reference app's exports (Inst C, Classic U, Inst SQC); others come untagged (D Classic, CT2F, and CPM35's `output_1.jpg`) and are taken as sRGB, which is how the phone shows an untagged JPEG and so what the owner holds RetroSnap against (the untagged and tagged exports share every JPEG parameter but the profile segment, so they are the same app's output through two paths; the EXIF colour-space tag is "uncalibrated" on both). The app's shaders work on sRGB values (SwiftUI's colour effects and the Core Image context both run in sRGB), so every picture is converted to sRGB before it is measured or fitted (`tools/fit.py` does it, and `tools/refcrop.py` keeps the profile on a crop so that it can; the pairs under `ref/pairs` are sRGB). The first fits read the P3 values as sRGB, which made every saturated colour come out duller on a phone than in the measurements; that is fixed, and the P3-valued pairs are kept under `ref/pairs/p3/`.

What a screenshot is good for: tone, colour, frames and layout, at screen resolution (the picture is about 900 px wide). What it is not good for: grain structure and fine texture, which need a saved export. An export (batch 2's references, and CPM35's second picture) is good for everything: the canvas is the whole file, grain and softness are measured at full resolution (`tools/grain.py`, `tools/softness.py`), and the stamp at its true size (`tools/stamp.py`). The result screenshots carry the reference app's banner and close button: crop to the picture before measuring. Whether the reference app's preview equals its export is still unknown for batch 1's cameras (no export of them); batch 2 is matched to the exports.

The first input is landscape and the other two portrait, so every canvas has been seen both ways: the reference app lays the strip, the 6×7 rebate, the compact's 3:2 crop and the mini print's sheet down for a landscape picture and stands them up for a portrait one, turns the slide mount's window but not the mount, and keeps the square frames and the square print the same way up whatever the picture. A film camera's date back turns with the film: it runs down the left edge of a portrait picture, as a real one would. "Frames" and "Stamps" below have the measurements.

## Pipeline work

New layers, and the batch that introduces each. A new layer is added in both renderers (`PhotoLookView` and `LookRenderer.render`, same order) and, if it needs a shader uniform, in all four of `RetroLook.metal`, `LookShaders`, `LookRenderer`, `LookUniforms`.

| Layer | Batch | What |
|---|---|---|
| lut | 1 ☑ | The colour is a measured 3D lookup table (`ColorCube`, `Resources/Looks/<id>.cube`, 17 nodes per axis, trilinear in gamma-encoded sRGB: `rsColorCube` on screen, `CIColorCube` in the bake), fitted by `tools/lut.py` from every pair at once as one sparse least-squares solve, held toward the parametric fit where the pictures showed nothing and kept smooth, with skin tones weighted. Under it, as its prior and the fallback when the file is missing, the parametric `ColorStage`: a 3×3 matrix with offsets, a monotone cubic tone curve per channel, then `HueTerms` (per-hue shift, saturation, lightness), fitted by `tools/fit.py` in sRGB. The table reaches the per-pixel floor of these screenshots (2.0 to 3.2 ΔE2000 over 4×4 boxes; the rest is texture and the reference's own spatial rendering) where the parametric fit stopped 0.3 to 0.8 above it, and it renders the face as the reference does, which the parametric fit could not (it left the face 2 to 4 L dark on every camera). The CSS `LookFilter` chain stays for the first five until they migrate in batches 2 and 6. |
| halation | 1 ☑ | `Halation`: the highlights above a threshold, tinted, blurred by a radius in points and screened over the picture (`rsHalationGlow`, and `.blur` / `CIGaussianBlur` + screen in the two renderers), fitted from the pairs by `tools/halation.py`: what the colour-only fit leaves unexplained next to bright pixels. On all five cameras the reference's glow is white and tight (threshold 0.6, radius 2 to 2.5 points, amount 0.15 to 0.4), not the orange halo batch 1 first drew by eye: the reference lifts what sits next to a highlight by 4 to 6 L on the selfie, the white glow recovers half of it, and the rest is the screen's own softness. |
| grain | 1 ☑ | `FilmGrain`: gradient noise with a clump size in points, an amplitude at mid grey that falls to nothing in black and white, and a mono-to-colour mix, added to the picture; the Grain slider scales the amplitude. The CSS `grain` opacity stays for the first five. |
| frame:strip | 1 ☑ | `LookFrame.filmStrip` + `LookFrameView`: real 135 film around the 3:2 picture, measured from the reference app's screens (see "Frames"), the DX code along one edge; lies down for a landscape picture, stands up for a portrait one. |

## Measuring a pair

The scripts live in `tools/` (Python; make a venv with `python3 -m venv venv && venv/bin/pip install numpy pillow scipy`, and run them with `venv/bin/python -I`). The loop for one camera:

1. `tools/refscan.py` and `tools/refcrop.py` find the picture inside the reference screenshot and save the crop, with its colour profile (the result screen's banner, close button and the camera's own canvas are outside it). For an export of a print, `tools/printscan.py` measures the sheet, the print and the window first (and the paper's colours, corners and shadow for the artwork), and the crop is the window; a bare 3:2 export is its own crop. `tools/stamp.py` measures a date back and prints the `--mask` that keeps it out of the fit.
2. `tools/fit.py <id> ref/photo-input/input.JPG <crop> ref/pairs` converts both pictures to sRGB, aligns the crop with the input (scale and offset, by the correlation of luminance gradients), writes the pair at the reference's resolution (`ref/pairs/<id>-input.png`, `<id>-reference.png`), fits the colour stage (matrix, curves, hue terms) plus the look's vignette by least squares on CIELAB differences, and writes `<id>.json` (the numbers for `CameraLook`) and `<id>-report.png` (input | fit | reference, with the curves). `--mask` keeps a date stamp out of the fit, `--inset` the mount's shadow, `--reg` pulls the matrix toward identity, `--hue-reg` the hue terms toward doing nothing.
3. `tools/sanity.py ref/pairs/<id>.json` runs the fit over a hue sweep and a grey ramp: a fit that swings a hue it never saw by tens of degrees wants more `--reg`.
4. Transcribe the JSON into the camera's `ColorStage` in `CameraLook.swift`. Then `tools/lut.py <id> ref/pairs --pair ref/pairs/<id> [--pair …] --halation <threshold,radius,amount,tint> [--weight …] --prior 0.3 --smooth 2 --skin 3` fits the table over the same pairs (after the camera's halation, since the table sits behind the glow in the pipeline), writes `ref/pairs/<id>.cube` and `<id>-lut.json`, and the `.cube` goes to `Retrosnap/Resources/Looks/<id>.cube` with `colorCube: "<id>"` on the recipe; `compare.py` then measures parity against the table.
5. `xcodebuild test … -only-testing:RetrosnapTests/ReferencePairTests` renders every pair through the real pipeline (`LookRenderer`, at the size the Viewer gives the picture) into `ref/pairs/<id>-retrosnap.png`. The test is skipped on a machine without `ref/`.
6. `tools/compare.py ref/pairs/<id>` prints the median and 95th percentile ΔE2000 of RetroSnap against the reference, of the fit against the reference, and of RetroSnap against the fit (pipeline parity). `tools/grain.py ref/pairs/<id>` measures the grain of the reference and of RetroSnap's render in the same flat blocks, and their ratio at mid grey: the reference's texture is what `FilmGrain` is tuned to. It also prints the detail each keeps where the input has some, in the middle and at the corners; `tools/softness.py <id> ref/pairs/<id> …` turns that into the camera's `blurRadius` (and says whether the corners want a `Softness` on top). `tools/halation.py ref/pairs/<id> ref/pairs/<id>-1 [--fine]` fits the camera's `Halation` from the same pairs: the glow the reference spreads around highlights, as the threshold, radius, amount and tint that best explain what the colour-only fit leaves next to bright pixels. `tools/stamp.py` run on RetroSnap's export checks a date back the same way it measured the reference's.

A camera with several pictures (`<id>`, `<id>-1`, `<id>-2`, … under "Re-verifying a shipped batch with a new picture"; since batch 2 every camera has three):

- Each new picture is cropped and aligned on its own with steps 1 and 2, under its pair name (`tools/fit.py <id>-<n> ref/photo-input/input_<n>.* <crop> ref/pairs …`). The single-picture fit this writes is only a bound: what the colour stage could reach on that picture alone. A canvas the reference app lays at an angle (the D Slide mount) is turned straight first with `tools/refcrop.py … --deskew`.
- The camera's fit is one colour stage over every pair at once: `tools/fit.py <id> <outdir> --pair ref/pairs/<id> --pair ref/pairs/<id>-1 --reg <r>`. Each pair counts the same whatever its size, so the recipe is as right on one picture as on the other; a pair's mask and inset come from its own JSON. When a camera cannot meet the tolerance on every picture, `--weight <pair>=w` leans the fit toward the picture that misses, and the fit kept is the one with the smallest worst median. The chosen fit's JSON and report are copied to `ref/pairs/<id>.json` and `<id>-report.png`, replacing the single-picture fit, so `tools/compare.py` measures parity against what is transcribed.
- `ReferencePairTests` renders every pair a camera has, and `tools/compare.py ref/pairs/<id> ref/pairs/<id>-1` and `tools/grain.py` measure each. Every pair must be within the tolerances.

Tolerances, set in batch 1 from what screenshots allow: **median ΔE2000 ≤ 3.0** for RetroSnap against the reference over the 4×4 box-filtered picture, **≤ 1.0** for RetroSnap against the Python fit (parity), and grain amplitude within a factor of 1.5 of the reference's high-pass standard deviation at mid grey. The grain left after the box filter is worth about 0.1 of the median on both sides of the comparison, so a camera at 3.0 ± 0.1 is at the line, not over it (batch 1 on its first picture: `negative` measured 3.02, the other four 1.8 to 2.7; after the second picture and the refit on both, four cameras measure 2.0 to 2.9 on both pictures and `negative` 3.5 and 3.6; after the move to sRGB and the hue terms, the fits are `plastic35` 2.4/2.3, `tlr` 1.9/2.6, `mediumFormat` 2.6/2.9, `slide` 3.5/2.5 and `negative` 3.4/3.6, each the fit with the smallest worse median). Half of what is left is texture, not colour: with both pictures blurred by 16 px the same fits measure 1.2 to 2.7, so the colour mapping is closer than the box-filtered median says. The floor for any per-pixel colour model on these screenshots, the scatter of the reference around the mean of each input colour, is 2.0 to 3.3; the measured tables (13 October) sit on it: `tlr` 1.7/2.3, `plastic35` 2.0/2.2, `mediumFormat` 2.2/2.6, `slide` 2.5/2.2, `negative` 2.6/3.2. A camera with several pictures must meet them on every picture. Batch 2, on three full-resolution exports each: `film` 1.6/1.5/1.8, `instSquare` 1.9/1.7/2.4, `compactPro` 2.4/2.6/3.0, `inst` 2.5/3.0/2.1, `classicSLR` 2.8/3.0/3.1 (the sign picture at the line, with grain about 0.1 of it); parity 0.5 to 0.7 for the film cameras and 0.9 to 1.15 for the two prints and Classic U, where the "fit" side of the comparison is the table on the sharp input while the render carries the camera's blur and grain, which is the caveat on that number for a blurred camera. Vignette fall-off is fitted as part of the colour stage (the look's radial gradient, with its amount as the free parameter). The screenshot is a preview at screen resolution; whether the reference app's export differs is unknown (D: no export available), so these numbers are against its preview.

### Re-verifying a shipped batch with a new picture

A batch is fitted on one pair, so its real accuracy is only known on a picture the fit never saw. To check one, import a second picture into the reference app, apply each camera, screenshot the result screen as before, and save the files as `ref/photo-input/input_<n>.*` and `ref/photo/<reference name>/output_<n>.*` (the phone's own extension). Pairs made from them are `ref/pairs/<id>-<n>-…`, and after a refit the camera's fit at `ref/pairs/<id>.json` is the one over every picture (the single-picture fits of batch 1 are kept under `ref/pairs/batch1/`). What the owner's third look taught (13 October 2026, "my face is more red, Dazz looks white"): whole-picture statistics hid it, because a face is a small region and the fit's compromise fell on it: every camera rendered the face 2 to 4 L darker than the reference.
